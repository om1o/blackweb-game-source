**ARACHNOPHOBIA: BLACKWEB — Phase 0 implementation report**

This is the Phase 0 evidence snapshot. The later Phase 1 source and portable package are recorded in [Phase1Progress.md](Phase1Progress.md).

Evidence snapshot: 2026-11-03. **The requested AAA-quality Phase 0 target has not been met.** This delivery contains the recovered house, an editable Godot implementation and a self-contained portable package. Automated progression/spider checks, both controller-driven routes, rendered integration checks and isolated portable-package checks passed. The optimized native entrance sample measures 26.653 ms median (about 37.5 FPS), but its 43.297 ms p95 and the automated encounter median of 38.244 ms do not establish stable 30 FPS across the opening. No human playtest or approval of the original quality bar is claimed.

The supplied material was a packaged Unreal game, not its editable source repository. The user's later instructions authorized asset reuse and a rebuild without Unreal, superseding the initial request to remain in the original engine. The active implementation therefore uses **Godot 4.7.2.stable.official.ed1daf0bf**, with the Compatibility renderer. No open world or infected return chapter was built. Earlier Unreal C++ scaffolding remains preserved but is not the active game and was not engine-compiled.

The implemented opening replaces gift collection with Mara's interrupted Greyhaven warning, keys and flashlight preparation, a presence beat, power loss, a short spider encounter, and an exit transition. Mara tells Luke to stay home; Luke decides to look for her. Dialogue currently appears as timed subtitles. **There is no performed Mara voice track.** The game does not explain BLACKWEB's full plot or build the next chapter.

The source includes contextual interaction prompts, required-item gating, checkpoint milestones, save validation and backup fallback, damage/recovery, restart and pause behavior. Interaction checks use player range and obstruction rays. Gift props, old instructions and the holiday spider hat are excluded from the new opening. Entrance barriers were removed, pickup heights were measured against actual mesh triangles, and solid wall panels behind the exit were replaced with sections of the same wall mesh to form an aperture. The original capture remains available for rebuilding or comparison. [Layout](../Godot/data/house_layout.json), [placement audit](../Tools/Recovery/opening-geometry-audit.json), [doorway proposal and measurements](../Tools/Recovery/doorway-cutout-proposal.json).

The visual implementation uses recovered geometry and textures, correct centimetre-to-metre and coordinate conversion, shared material resources, corrected normal-map conventions, and practical lights that fade with the story state. Glass, foliage and lace receive documented material approximations. Small clutter is selectively excluded from collision. The latest observed scene-build report records 877/877 placements, 616 collision bodies and 226,906 collision triangles. Material overrides survive save/reload. This is structural evidence, **not proof of final visual fidelity**; the report explicitly marks material fidelity incomplete. [House import report](../Godot/data/house_import_report.json).

Luke uses the interim Microsoft Rocketbox Male_Adult_04 character. Its 81-bone rig and idle/walk/run animations were imported, grounded to a 1.75 m standing height, oriented toward Godot -Z and stripped of horizontal motion-extraction tracks. Source skinning and bound animation paths were validated. The character is recognizable third-person art, but is not a bespoke approximately 20-year-old Luke and does not yet have authored reaching, pickup, flashlight-holding or door-contact animations. There is no hand IK. [Luke asset details](LukeAsset.md), [validation](../Godot/assets/luke/validation.json).

The third-person controller includes acceleration, visual turning, a collision-aware spring arm, restrained shoulder framing and an equipment-gated flashlight. The character hides when camera obstruction would expose its interior. Movement clips follow achieved horizontal velocity, so pressing into a wall does not keep Luke walking in place. The final rendered route was reviewed; broader room coverage, camera comfort and object alignment still need human testing. The flashlight is camera-mounted. Its final range and shadow bias were adjusted after rendered inspection exposed distant shadow acne; its appearance and performance are not certified as showcase quality.

The spider preserves the recovered mesh, rig, textures and source motion. The source Idle sequence has RateScale 0, so idle is held as a static pose rather than misrepresented as a new idle animation. Walk and Run retain the recovered movement; the encounter uses anticipation, stalking, windup, a committed lunge, retreat, cooldown and a reachable safe region. Damage requires both visibility and a collision sweep. **Movement uses local obstacle steering, not a baked navigation mesh.** No procedural leg placement, surface climbing, bespoke attack/stagger/death animation or polished foot-contact system was added. These remain significant gaps against the brief. [Encounter implementation](../Godot/scripts/spider.gd), [source animation metadata](../RecoveredAssets/Arachnophobia/Content/Spider/animation-source-metadata.json).

Audio combines recovered wood footsteps, a restrained spatial presence cue and the original door creak with newly authored procedural mechanical hum, phone notification and power-cut sounds. Presence audio is limited to the tension beat and stops with power loss; there is no continuous horror score. Footsteps follow grounded movement distance. Newly authored procedural spider contacts use sparse dry taps tied to actual grounded travel, plus a brief scratch on attack windup; they contain no external samples and silence on pause, recovery and completion. These cues are not recovered source audio or per-toe IK. [Spider audio](SpiderAudio.md). There is no full room-by-room soundscape, recorded Mara performance or complete surface-dependent footstep set. [Soundscape source](../Godot/scripts/soundscape.gd).

The completed keys-first run walked the recovered house using the actual player controller, selected the phone and pickups through camera/reach checks, triggered Mara through E interaction, observed preparation and power loss, enabled the owned flashlight, entered the physical spider encounter, retreated to the safe region and reached the Greyhaven transition through the opening door. It used collision-audited routes and automated inputs; it was not human playtesting. The later rendered flashlight-first route also passed fatal damage/retry and pause/restart checks, 29/29 overall. The final optimized production rerun also passed 29/29 checks after batching, the achieved-velocity animation correction, the flashlight shadow adjustment and the practical-light optimization.

The final configuration keeps **native 1280 × 720 rendering with 2× MSAA** on the Compatibility renderer. On this machine Godot uses ANGLE/OpenGL ES 3.0 through Direct3D11 on Intel HD Graphics 530. Static batching groups 232 eligible instances into 78 spatial batches while retaining the original geometry and collisions. The optimized default disables the two practical room shadow maps while retaining illumination and the flashlight shadow; repeated light-property writes stop when power is steady. The original room-shadow flags remain available through `--room-shadows`. [Optimization report and launch option](OptimizationReport.md).

Entrance draw calls fell 67.5%, from 496 to 161. **Stable 30 FPS across the opening is not established**: the entrance p95 exceeds 33.33 ms, and the encounter remains slower. Room lighting contrast changes because practical lights no longer produce object shadows; flashlight shadows remain. [Controlled comparison](../Evidence/perf-shadow-aba.json).
